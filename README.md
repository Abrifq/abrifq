### Rambling a bit more about open source

Open source apps' claims can be verified by unaffiliated third parties,
which can give us a better comfort than just "trust the corporate who sells ads,
who would _neeever_ lie about harvesting and selling **our** data."

However, that does not mean we should sacrifice all the creature-comfort and
convenience features just because the app is open source and we are private.

People should be able to choose what conveniences they want,
see what tradeoffs they get by enabling those convenient features
and make their own decision.
They could even host their systems/services or use the project's _official_
services and fund them directly without feeling they have wasted money.

Again, it's about letting people choose.

If they want to move out, they should be able to, too.
We are fighting for freedom of options, freedom of **us**,
so we should still be able to work on a document via a different app,
use our media program in another platform,
use our chat apps in another server,
use our backup apps in our own servers for god's sake!

#### Lost the plot but here I ramble about backups, lol

</summary>

Dude, we have lost the plot of owning your data so badly by letting
phone manufacturers have their isolation-maxxing era (sue me, like i care)
and the apps themselves not playing nice with the users
-- because WHY WOULD THEY WHEN THEY CAN JUST HARVEST MORE, SAY IT'S DELETED OR
"ANONYMIZED" AND SELL IT TO DATA BROKERS, RIGHT?? --
that we can't even have backups of our local data on most things.

Most. Games are usually a bit better on this type of stuff compared to chat apps,
but they are still guilty nonetheless. So, again, embrace open source as that allows
you to know that:

1. you will be able to see what is being harvested and what is not
2. have an actual backup feature that works
3. and if it doesn't work, you _kiinda_ have someone to pester about.

	(If you are going to bother a developer for a feature request,
	then make sure to show your love when things are progressing/are done.
	Donate money, compute, or do your best friend-to-friend marketing.)

I would love a backup manager/orchestrator app to be coordinating
with your backup service and your app's backup api.

It wouldn't have to use plain backups, once again, it can be encrypted.

It could even have a (also encrypted) metadata about backup state so
it can even provide partial backups for data and time saving
WHILE not compromising data safety.

The app will be just the middleman, as most apps are in deep sleep most of the time
and this app should activate on a specific time of the day like an alarm app.

Even on an offline scenario, since the apps themselves can keep
their last backup metadata, it can provide a hash to check against
the server's backup metadata and also provide a partial backup to
the orchestrator/manager app to use/send if the metadata still matches.

Please note that, I am rambling about stuff, so there is no implementation,
and there is no concrete example we could easily refer, so it is only in theory.
As always, it will be harder in practice.
